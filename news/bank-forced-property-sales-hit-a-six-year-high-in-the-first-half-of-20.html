Bank-Forced Property Sales Hit a Six-Year High in the First Half of 20262026年上半年强制售屋案例创六年新高

The number of mortgagee sales in Singapore, where a bank forces a property to be sold after an owner falls behind on repayments, hit a six-year high in the first half of 2026. The last time the count was this elevated was in 2020, at the height of early pandemic disruption.

Tighter financing conditions are behind the rise. Higher interest rates over recent years have pushed up monthly mortgage repayments, and some owners who bought at peak prices are finding it increasingly hard to keep up. When a borrower defaults and cannot reach an arrangement with their lender, the property gets sold at whatever the market will bear, sometimes at a discount to the going rate.

For buyers, a higher volume of mortgagee sales can occasionally surface properties at below-market prices, though these deals carry their own complications and need careful due diligence. More broadly, a pickup in forced sales adds a layer of modest downward pressure on prices in certain segments. If you are an owner feeling the strain on your mortgage, speak to your bank early, before the situation reaches a point where you lose control of the outcome entirely.

新加坡的抵押权人强制售屋（mortgagee sale）案例，即屋主拖欠还款后被银行强制出售房产，在2026年上半年创下六年新高。上一次达到这个水平，还是2020年疫情初期最混乱的时候。

背后的主要原因是融资条件收紧。这几年利率走高，每月的房贷还款额也随之上涨，部分在房价高峰期入市的屋主，如今越来越难以应付。一旦借款人无力还款、又无法与银行达成任何协议，房产就会被以市场能接受的价格出售，有时甚至低于市场价。

对买家来说，强制售屋案例增多，偶尔能让人以低于市价的价格买到房产，但这类交易本身有不少复杂之处，需要仔细做好尽职调查。从大局来看，强制售屋案例增加，也会对部分市场领域的价格带来一定的下行压力。如果你是屋主，觉得房贷压力越来越大，请尽早和银行沟通，不要等到局面完全失控才行动。

What it means for you对你意味着什么
More owners are struggling to service their loans, which could bring distressed properties to market and put modest pressure on prices in some segments.越来越多屋主难以偿还贷款，这可能会把更多困难房产推向市场，并对部分房产类别的价格造成一定的下行压力。
